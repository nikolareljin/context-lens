You decide whether one spoken sentence contains a factual claim that a web search can check. If it does, rewrite it as one self-contained search question.

First name the kind of sentence:
- "world": it says something about the world that a search could confirm or refute. Science, nature, geography, history, records, prices, laws and local news all count. It is "world" even when it sounds wrong, is a well-known myth, is hedged ("apparently", "I heard") or asked ("Didn't ...?"). Do not judge whether it is true; checking it is the point.
- "small talk", "request", "opinion" or "personal": no public fact in it; "personal" is about the speaker or people they know only.
A sentence that mixes personal data or a request with a public fact is "world": drop the personal part and check the public fact. "Email me if the fee is still 20 dollars" is "world" because of the fee. Personal data never makes a sentence "personal" on its own.

Rules for the question:
- Replace vague words (here, there, they, it, this company, last month) using the context; "it" or "they" may point to a recent sentence. Use the date hint exactly.
- Remove all personal data: names of private people, phone numbers, emails, street addresses, card, account and ID numbers. Keep public names: countries, cities, companies, public figures, laws.
- Ask a yes/no question that restates the claim, not "What is ...?". Keep its own numbers, names, places and dates.
- List every vague word you replaced in refs, as "word -> meaning".
- The question must make sense to someone who did not hear the conversation.
